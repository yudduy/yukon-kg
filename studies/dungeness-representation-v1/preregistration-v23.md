# Version 23 Qualification Preregistration

Version 23 changes seed production only. R0, R1, R2, treatment prompts, chain caps, outcomes, and the $500 study ceiling remain unchanged.

Each seed is one campaign of at most six fresh sessions. The campaign shares only the current clean-verified candidate. Producers never receive captured events, a research view, narrative resume state, treatment labels, or outer scores. Every session—including a crash or zero-evaluation session—is retained and consumes the shared cap.

Campaign cap: $6 normalized model cost, 400,000 model tokens, 3,600 agent seconds, eight development evaluations, and six sessions. Session slice: at most $3, 200,000 tokens, 600 seconds, and two evaluations; the final slice is reduced to remaining aggregate capacity. The per-session values are reservation envelopes, not fresh aggregate budgets: sequential sessions still stop at $6 and 400,000 actual normalized usage. The 200,000-token envelope is required by the installed Codex schema and conservative in-flight reservation: the official-Moonshot canary used only 21,543 actual tokens but could not reserve its fourth request within 100,000, before its first `evaluate`. A campaign is history-eligible only with at least four valid numeric evaluations, two distinct candidate hashes, and two distinct scores. Only the best clean-verified candidate observed across all sessions advances; a later regression cannot replace it. The existing frozen seed-convergence checker additionally requires positive progress and at least one meaningful gain of headroom.

The first timing-based task was removed before any seed history or treatment run. Three unchanged-candidate clean scores for `agent_tool_routing` spanned 3.240754–3.334545 seconds, or 0.272 meaningful-gain units, which is wider than the 0.25-unit practical effect. It remains apparatus-canary evidence only. The pre-existing deterministic reserve rule selects `adversarial_splay`; two current full Harbor smokes returned exactly 48,656 rotations with zero model use. Its declared reference is 67,008 rotations.

The two seed histories for each task are now independent GPT campaigns rather than one GPT and one Kimi campaign. This change was made before any eligible history or treatment existed. Three exact Kimi endpoints failed their bounded canaries: Together became unavailable; the official Moonshot endpoint returned an in-budget provider 429; Parasail completed 11 requests but then became unavailable. These are route-availability failures, not evidence about representations. Keeping a volatile producer would make history identity depend on provider churn. Each A/B history instead has a distinct frozen block identity, fresh sessions, separate campaign lineage, and no shared post-start evidence.

The first five qualification campaigns, in frozen order, are adversarial-splay/GPT-A, adversarial-splay/GPT-B, toy-ISA/GPT-A, toy-ISA/GPT-B, and VLIW/GPT-A. C-1 passes if at least four of five are eligible without a protocol or apparatus failure. VLIW/GPT-B is then produced to complete six pilot histories. A provider outage may trigger one explicitly linked administrative replacement; task behavior does not. Kimi remains a conditional consumer-model replication only after a stable exact route is separately frozen; it cannot launch automatically.

Before the first campaign: full tests pass, the active GPT public route policy matches the live catalog, every task passes internal-only bundle preflight and model-free Docker smoke, and the GPT producer route completes a bounded canary with at least one model request and one tool call without a provider error. Inactive OpenRouter routes are not carried in the live policy: this prevents an unavailable, unused endpoint from blocking or silently changing a GPT-only run. Route replacement is permitted only before an eligible history exists, must pin one exact endpoint with fallbacks disabled, and must repeat its canary. Confirmation remains separately authorized and cannot start automatically.
